Get your file

  1. On a computer, sign in at strava.com (the bulk export isn't in the phone app).
  2. Hover over your name (top right) and choose Settings, then the My Account tab.
  3. Under Download your account, click Get Started, then Request download.
  4. Strava emails you a link, usually within a few hours. Download the .zip and drop it here as is. Big archives with photos are fine: only activities.csv and your route files are read.

Source: Strava Support: Exporting your Data. On a phone? You can unzip it and upload just activities.csv (no route art then).

  1. On a computer, sign in at connect.garmin.com and open Activities → All Activities.
  2. Scroll down until the whole year has loaded. Garmin only exports the rows you can see.
  3. Click Export CSV (top right of the list) and upload Activities.csv here.
  4. Want route art too? Export single activities as GPX (activity → gear icon → Export to GPX) and add them along with the CSV.

Garmin's full "Export Your Data" archive (Account → Data Management) also works: drop the .zip and we read its activity summary file.

Your activities never leave your device

There's no server to send them to

This page is plain files on GitHub Pages. Your export is read, counted and drawn into cards by JavaScript in this browser tab. Close the tab and it's gone.

The browser blocks it anyway

The page ships a Content-Security-Policy that only allows one outside address: the optional email signup, which sends just your email. No analytics, no trackers, no map tiles, no web fonts.

Routes are drawn, not mapped

Route art is plain line drawings with no map and no coordinates. By default we also cut 500 m around every start and finish, so your front door isn't on your story.

Try it offline

Load this page, turn on airplane mode, then add your export. It still works. The code is open on GitHub.

Waiting on your export?

Optional. Get one email reminding you to come back and make your cards, plus a note when next year's edition is ready. We only send your email address; nothing from your activities is ever included.

Questions

Is this made by Strava or Garmin?

No. Year in Motion is an independent project that reads the export files Strava and Garmin already let you download. It is not affiliated with, endorsed by or connected to Strava or Garmin, and it doesn't use either company's API.

Is it really free?

Yes. Every card is free. A printable poster may come later as an optional extra.

Why are some cards missing?

Cards only appear when your data supports them. Route art needs GPS files (the Strava .zip, or GPX/FIT/TCX files), so a CSV alone won't have it. "Fastest efforts" only shows runs close to 5K, 10K, half or full marathon distance, and rides of 20 km or more. It uses whole-activity averages, so it's a rough guide rather than an official PR.

My times of day look off

Strava's export stores start times in UTC. We convert them with your phone's time zone, or with the zone your default activity names ("Morning Run", "Evening Ride") point to. Activities recorded while traveling may land an hour or two off.

My Garmin distances look wrong

Garmin's CSV uses your account's units without saying which. We guess, and you can switch it under Adjust the cards ("Distances in your Garmin file are in").

How is the sport personality picked?

From your numbers alone: streaks, how many weeks you were active, weekend share, start times, climbing per distance, average session length, sport variety, commutes and how many different places you started from. The strongest signal wins.
