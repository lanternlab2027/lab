How the report card works

Where do the numbers come from?

From the U.S. Social Security Administration's national baby-name files, which count first names on Social Security card applications for babies born in the U.S., by year and sex, from 1880 to 2025. SSA only lists a name in a year when at least 5 babies of one sex got it, so very rare names and spellings are missing, and the totals here are sums of the published names, not all births. This tool includes every name given in 2025 plus any name with 200+ babies since 1880.

How is "rank" counted?

Rank is within one sex for one year, exactly as SSA orders its files: most babies first, ties broken alphabetically. A name used for both boys and girls has two ranks; the card shows the sex it's given to more often.

Why "share of births" instead of raw counts?

Far more babies are born now than in 1880, so raw counts flatter recent years. The chart and the peak year use babies per million births of that sex. "1 in 105 girls" means that share in that year.

How are Rising, Falling, Classic and Vintage revival decided?

All on share of births, in this order:

  • Vintage revival: some decade from the 1880s to the 1940s averaged 300+ per million, a decade from the 1950s to the 2000s dropped to a quarter of that or less, and the last five years are back to at least 3x that low point (and 100+ per million).
  • Classic: given every year since 1880, still 300+ per million, and its busiest decade was no more than 8x its quietest (16x for names still in roughly the top 150).
  • Rising / Falling: the last five years vs the same five years a decade earlier, up 25%+ or down 20%+.
  • Falling (long-term): level lately, but under a tenth of its best decade.
  • Steady: everything else.
How are "similar names on the rise" picked?

A simple spelling rule, not a pronunciation dictionary: names one letter away from yours (add, drop or change one letter), two letters away if they start with the same letter and both have 5+ letters, or names that share your first two and last two letters. Only names given to 100+ babies in 2025 whose share of births grew 30%+ over the last five years qualify. Same-sex names come first, then the closest spellings, then the fastest growers.

Does this track me?

No. We count anonymous page views with GoatCounter (no cookies, no personal data). No ad trackers, no third-party scripts or fonts. Name lookups load small data files from this site. Besides the counter, the only outside address the page may contact is the optional email signup below, which sends just your email.

Get the new rankings

SSA publishes each year's names once a year, usually in May. Get one email when the 2026 rankings are in the report card.
