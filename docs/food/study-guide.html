The exam in brief

The final exam has 50 multiple-choice questions, lasts one hour, and you need 70% or more to pass. The free online course has 15 lessons; the exam costs $24. The certificate never expires. (DOHMH course details) (DOHMH Food Protection Online Course; Online course FAQ p. 1)

Temperatures to know

  • The Temperature Danger Zone is between 41°F and 140°F. Potentially hazardous foods left in it for more than 2 hours are no longer safe. (Study Guide p. 1; Study Guide p. 8)
  • Hold cold foods at or below 41°F, except smoked fish at or below 38°F. Keep frozen foods at 0°F or lower. Hold hot foods at 140°F or higher. (Study Guide p. 2; Training Manual p. 85; Study Guide p. 5)

Cooking, reheating and thawing

  • Minimum cooking temperatures: poultry, stuffed meat and stuffing 165°F; ground meat 158°F; pork 150°F; raw shell eggs 145°F; fish, shellfish, beef, lamb and all other meats 140°F. (Study Guide p. 5; Study Guide p. 4)
  • Reheat previously cooked foods rapidly to 165°F on a stove or in an oven. Never use a hot holding unit to reheat. (Study Guide p. 5)
  • Thaw in the refrigerator, under cold running water, or in a microwave followed by continuous cooking. Never thaw at room temperature. (Study Guide p. 5; Training Manual p. 88)
  • Never use bare hands with ready-to-eat foods. (Study Guide p. 5)

Cooling

  • Cool from 140°F to 70°F within two hours, then from 70°F to 41°F within four more hours. (Training Manual p. 87)
  • Pour food 1 to 2 inches deep into 4-inch-deep pans, or cut solid foods into pieces of 6 lbs. or less. Cover foods only after they have cooled to 41°F or below. (Study Guide p. 5)

Receiving and storage

  • Keep shellfish tags on file for at least 90 days after the product is used. (Study Guide p. 1)
  • Pasteurized milk has sell-by dates of 9 days; ultra-pasteurized, 45 days. (Study Guide p. 1)
  • Store all food at least 6 inches off the floor, and never under wastewater lines. Use FIFO: First In, First Out. (Study Guide p. 2)
  • Store raw foods below cooked foods. Raw meats, bottom to top: poultry, ground meats, pork, all other meats. (Study Guide p. 2; Training Manual p. 89)

Hazards and microbes

  • The three main hazards are physical, chemical and biological. (Study Guide p. 3)
  • Six factors affect bacterial growth: Food, Acidity, Temperature, Time, Oxygen and Moisture (FATTOM). Under favorable conditions, bacteria can double every 20 to 30 minutes. (Study Guide p. 3)
  • Hepatitis A and norovirus are two common food-borne viruses. Staphylococcus aureus is commonly carried by healthy people. Smoked fish is held at 38°F to prevent the growth of Clostridium botulinum. (Study Guide p. 3; Study Guide p. 4; Study Guide p. 1)

Personal hygiene

  • Hand washing sinks must be within 25 feet of all food preparation areas and in or near all toilets, with soap, hot and cold running water, disposable towels or a hand dryer, and a “Wash Hands” sign. (Study Guide p. 4)
  • Gloves never replace good hand washing. Workers wear no jewelry on arms and hands except wedding bands and medical bracelets. (Training Manual p. 90; Study Guide p. 4)
  • Food workers with an illness that can spread through food should not work until fully recovered. (Study Guide p. 4)

Cleaning and sanitizing

  • Wash dishes by hand in this order: wash, rinse, sanitize, air-dry. Manual dishwashing is allowed only in a three-compartment sink. (Study Guide p. 7; Training Manual p. 92)
  • Hot-water sanitizing: immerse utensils in 170°F water for at least 30 seconds. A high-temperature dishwasher's final rinse must be at least 180°F. (Study Guide p. 7; Training Manual p. 92)
  • 50 PPM chlorine solution is ½ ounce of bleach per gallon of water, for immersing utensils at least 1 minute and for storing wiping cloths. 100 PPM is 1 ounce per gallon, typically for wiping, spraying or pouring. Check chemical solutions with a test kit. (Study Guide p. 7)

Pests and HACCP

  • The three key strategies of Integrated Pest Management are starve them, build them out and destroy them. Only a licensed pest control officer may apply insecticides and rodenticides. (Study Guide p. 7)
  • HACCP stands for Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Point. It has seven principles, and a CCP is any point in the food flow where action must be taken to eliminate the hazard. (Study Guide p. 8)
