    How we estimate

    This is a simple points table, not artificial intelligence. Everything it uses comes from the National Weather Service forecast for the 2.5 km grid square around your location, and every number is below so you can check our math.

    1. The window: 6pm tonight to 8am tomorrow

    We add up the NWS forecast for snow and ice from 6pm the evening before until 8am on the school morning, in your local time. After midnight and before 5am, “tomorrow” means the morning that's coming up.

    Alert points are added together but capped at 30. Alerts count if they're active for your point and haven't ended before tonight.

    3. Adjustments

    • Timing. If the snow stops before 5am (less than 0.1 in. falls from 5am to 8am), the overnight snow counts at 60%, because crews have time to plow before buses run.
    • Confidence. Snow and ice points are multiplied by 0.6 + 0.4 × min(1, rain-or-snow chance ÷ 70%), using the highest NWS chance of precipitation in the window.

    4. The chance

    chance = snow + ice + cold + alerts, rounded and kept between 1% and 99%. We never say 0% or 100%: districts surprise everyone.

    Example: 6 in. of snow still falling at 6am in a snow-used town with a Winter Storm Warning is 50 + 20 = 70%. The same 6 in. near Atlanta maxes out the snow points (75) for 95%.

    Why you pick “where you live”

    The same 3 inches means different things in Buffalo and Atlanta. Places that get snow every winter have the plows, salt and habits to keep going; places that rarely see it often close for an inch or a glaze of ice. We pre-select a guess based on how far north you are, but you know your town better.

    What it doesn't know

    Your district's road crews, bus routes, rural vs. city roads, how many snow days are left in the calendar, and holidays. Forecasts also change; check again in the evening and the morning.
