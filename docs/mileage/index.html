Google Maps Timeline mileage log

Turn your Google Maps Timeline export into a business mileage log: every drive with date, start, end and miles. Tag trips business or personal, name the places you go often, and download a CSV with totals.

Your location history never leaves your device. No uploads, no account.

The IRS asks for records made at or near the time of each trip, with the mileage, date, destination and business purpose (IRS Publication 463, chapter 5, "Timely kept records" and Table 5-1). A reconstructed log may not meet that standard on its own. Check every trip against your calendar, invoices or receipts, add the business purpose, and ask a tax professional if you're unsure.

2. Name your frequent places

Trip ends within about 150 m (500 ft) of each other are grouped as one place. Names you type here show up in the log and the CSV. Driving between home and your regular workplace is commuting, which is personal (Pub 463, chapter 4).

How to export your Google Maps Timeline

Google now keeps Timeline on your phone, so the export comes from the phone. These steps are from Google's help page, Manage your Google Maps Timeline ("Export your Timeline data").

Android

  1. Open your phone's Settings app.
  2. Tap Location → Location services → Timeline.
  3. Under "Timeline," tap Export Timeline data, then Continue.
  4. Pick where to save it and tap Save. The file is usually called Timeline.json.

iPhone and iPad

  1. Open the Google Maps app.
  2. Tap your profile picture or initial → Settings → Location & Privacy.
  3. Tap Export Timeline data.
  4. On the share sheet, tap Save to Files, pick a folder and tap Save. The file is location-history.json.

Then open this page on the same phone and choose the file, or move it to a computer. Older Google Takeout downloads also work: use the month files in Location History (Timeline) → Semantic Location History, or the whole .zip. Records.json alone has no trips in it. Timeline only has trips if it was turned on at the time, and auto-delete (3, 18 or 36 months) can remove old ones, so export before tax season.

How it works

  • On your device. The file is read by this page in your browser. There's no server and no upload. You can turn on airplane mode after the page loads and it still works.
  • Drives only. Google labels each movement with a guess (driving, walking, cycling, bus…). You choose which types count.
  • Miles. Google's own distance for each trip is used. If a trip has none, the miles are estimated from the path points recorded during it, or as a straight line between the ends (which undercounts). Those trips are marked in the CSV.
  • IRS rate. The estimate uses the IRS business standard mileage rate for each trip's date. For 2026 that's 72.5¢ a mile through June 30 and 76¢ from July 1 (IRS: Standard mileage rates).

Questions

Is a Google Maps Timeline mileage log enough for the IRS?

Maybe not on its own. IRS Publication 463 says a record made at or near the time of the trip carries more weight than one prepared later, and the log needs the business purpose of each trip. Use this as a starting point, check it against your calendar and receipts, and talk to a tax professional. This page is not tax advice.

Why are some trips missing or wrong?

Timeline is a guess built from your phone's location. Trips can be split, merged, mislabeled as walking, or missing if location was off. Turn on more trip types above, and fix what you find before you rely on the totals.

Does it work with the new on-device Timeline?

Yes. It reads the Android export (Timeline.json, with semanticSegments), the iPhone export (location-history.json) and the older Takeout Semantic Location History month files.

Is anything saved?

Nothing leaves your device. Only if you tick "Remember my place names and tags," your names and tags (not your location history) are kept in this browser's storage so you can pick up later. Untick it to erase them.
