    How Chicago restaurant inspections work

    Chicago does not give restaurants letter grades. Inspectors from the Chicago Department of Public Health (CDPH) Food Protection Program record a result for each inspection, and each establishment has a risk category.

    Inspection results

    ResultWhat CDPH says it means
    PassNo priority or priority foundation violations were noted during the inspection.1,2
    Pass with Conditions
    (“Pass w/ Conditions” in the data)    		One or more priority or priority foundation violations were found but were corrected on site during the inspection.1,2
    FailOne or more priority or priority foundation violations were found that could not be corrected during the inspection.1,2 CDPH re-inspects after the establishment has had time to correct them.2

    The data also contains records labeled Out of Business, No Entry, Not Ready and Business Not Located. These are not inspection results, and we couldn't find an official CDPH definition for them, so we show them by their labels only and point to the last completed inspection.

    Risk categories 1, 2 and 3
    CDPH assigns each establishment a risk level of 1 (High), 2 (Medium) or 3 (Low).3 It's based on the type of food and how it is prepared (an FDA model), and it is reassessed at every full annual inspection.2 It's about the kind of food, not how the place performed. CDPH says risk 1 establishments are inspected twice a year, risk 2 once a year, and risk 3 every other year.3
    Priority, priority foundation and core violations
    Priority (critical) violations can lead directly to foodborne illness; priority foundation (serious) violations support or make possible things that can lead to it; core (minor) violations are usually general sanitation or minor repair issues.2 The data doesn't label each violation's level, so we only show one when the inspector's own comment names it.
    Inspection types
    Canvass (routine, generally unannounced), complaint, re-inspection (follow-up) and license inspections, among others.2 Complaints can come in through 311; a complaint being filed doesn't mean the condition exists.2
    Results are a snapshot
    Results reflect the findings at the time of the inspection. Violations are allegations that can be disputed at the Department of Administrative Hearings.4 Results are usually posted within 2–3 weeks of an inspection.1
    July 2018 rule change
    Chicago changed its food inspection procedures on July 1, 2018, including the violation list, so older inspections use different violation numbers.5
