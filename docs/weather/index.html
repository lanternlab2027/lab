How Weather Wrapped works

These numbers come from a weather reanalysis, a computer model's best estimate for a grid square of roughly 10 to 25 km around your location, so they can differ from your airport weather station's official records.

Type a ZIP code or a city. We look up its location, then your browser asks Open-Meteo's Historical Weather API for one value per day: the high and low temperature, precipitation, snowfall, sunshine and peak wind. Everything on the cards is counted from those daily values, in your browser.

2026 so far

Data runs only through yesterday, and the newest few days can lag while the data catch up. Until the year is over, cards say “2026 so far” and show the last day included. The full year is ready on Jan 1.

What each card counts

  • Hottest and coldest: the highest daily high and the lowest daily low.
  • Rain and snow: total precipitation (rain plus snow melted into water) and total snowfall. A wet day has at least 1 mm (0.04 in.).
  • Streaks: the most days in a row with less than 1 mm (dry), or at least 1 mm (wet). A missing day breaks a streak.
  • Sunniest month: the most hours of sunshine per day, among months with at least 20 days of data.
  • Hot days and freezing nights: days with a high of 90°F (32.2°C) or more, and nights with a low below 32°F (0°C).
  • The weirdest day: the day whose high was furthest from normal. “Normal” for a date is the average high over the 10 years before (2016–2025 for 2026), across the same date ±7 days, from the same source and grid point. If that comparison can't load, the card is skipped.
  • Summary: all of it on one card, plus the windiest day and how this year's highs compare with the 10-year average.
