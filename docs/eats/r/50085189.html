How NYC restaurant grades work

The city's Health Department (DOHMH) inspects restaurants unannounced, at least once a year. Each violation adds points, so a lower score is better: 0 to 13 points is an A, 14 to 27 a B, and 28 or more a C.

An initial inspection is graded only if it earns an A. Otherwise DOHMH returns for a re-inspection, typically within a month, and grades that. A restaurant that gets a B or C may post a Grade Pending card (code Z) until its hearing at the Office of Administrative Trials and Hearings. Code P is a Grade Pending card issued when a restaurant re-opens after a closure, and N means not yet graded. Scores can change after a hearing.

One inspection is a snapshot of one visit on one day. It doesn't show how a kitchen runs on other days, and it is not a rating of the food or service.

Sources: DOHMH, How We Score and Grade and Letter Grading for Restaurants; the grade codes are from the dataset's documentation.
