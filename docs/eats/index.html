    How NYC restaurant grades work

    The NYC Health Department (DOHMH) inspects restaurants unannounced. Each violation adds points, and the lower the score, the better the grade.1

    ScoreGrade
    0 to 13 pointsA
    14 to 27 pointsB
    28 or more pointsC
    Initial inspection and re-inspection
    A restaurant gets two chances to earn an A in each inspection cycle. If the initial inspection scores 0–13, that's an A. If not, the initial inspection is scored but not graded, and an inspector returns unannounced, typically within a month, for a re-inspection, which is graded A, B or C.1 DOHMH's data notes say the re-inspection is at least 7 days after the initial one.3
    Grade Pending (Z) and P
    A restaurant that gets a B or C on re-inspection receives both the grade card and a Grade Pending card, and can post either until it has a hearing at the Office of Administrative Trials and Hearings (OATH).1 In the data, Z means Grade Pending and P means a Grade Pending card issued when a restaurant re-opens after being closed following an initial inspection.2
    Not Yet Graded (N)
    Until a restaurant has a graded inspection, it is listed as Not Yet Graded.1 New restaurants with no inspection yet appear in the data with a placeholder date of January 1, 1900.3
    Critical vs. not critical
    Points depend on the health risk. DOHMH sorts violations into public health hazards (at least 7 points, for example not keeping food at the right temperature), critical violations (at least 5 points) and general violations (at least 2 points), with extra points for how extensive the problem is.1 The open data marks each violation Critical (the kind most likely to contribute to foodborne illness) or Not Critical; administrative and special-program items (such as smoke-free or menu-labeling rules) are marked Not Applicable and don't count toward the score.2,3
    Not every inspection is graded
    Compliance inspections, monitoring inspections, re-opening inspections, inspections of new restaurants not yet open, and some complaint inspections are scored but not graded.1,3 So the newest inspection isn't always the one behind the current grade.
    Scores can change after a hearing
    Restaurants can contest violations at OATH. Dismissed violations are removed from the data, and scores are updated after adjudication, so a score or grade here can change later.3
