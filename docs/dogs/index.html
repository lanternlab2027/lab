How the report card works

Where do the numbers come from?

From the NYC Department of Health and Mental Hygiene's dog licensing data, published on NYC Open Data (the "NYC Dog Licensing Dataset"). It lists every active dog license in each yearly extract: the dog's name, sex, birth year, breed and the owner's ZIP code, with no owner names or addresses. Licenses here were issued from 2014 to 2025.

Licensed dogs only, not all dogs. Many NYC dogs are never licensed, so these counts undercount every name. Treat a rank as "most common among licensed dogs", not "most popular".

How are dogs counted once?

A dog can appear many times: once for each license, and again in every yearly extract that still lists that license. We count one dog per unique name + birth year + breed + ZIP code. Two different dogs that match on all four are counted once, and a dog that moved ZIP codes or whose breed was typed differently on renewal is counted twice. Placeholder names such as "Unknown", "Name not provided", "N/A" and "None" are dropped.

What do the trend and the chart show?

The chart shows how many dogs per 10,000 licensed that year got a license with this name, by year the license was issued (2015–2025; 2014 starts in September, so it's left out). Using a share, not a raw count, matters because the city's yearly extracts don't capture every year equally.

Rising: the last three years' share is 25%+ above the first three. Fading: 20%+ below. Newcomer: absent from the first three years. Otherwise Steady.

Why can't I find my dog's name?

To protect owners' privacy, a name needs at least 5 licensed dogs to show up, and any breed, borough or ZIP code with fewer than 5 dogs of that name is hidden. Static name pages need 20+ dogs. Names are matched ignoring case, spaces, hyphens and periods ("Jay-Jay" = "Jay Jay").

Does this track me?

No. No analytics, no cookies, no third-party scripts or fonts. Lookups load small data files from this site.
