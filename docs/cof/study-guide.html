The exam format

Each exam is a timed, computer-based multiple-choice test with four answer choices per question, and you need at least 70% to pass. According to the booklets, F-01 has 20 questions in 30 minutes, F-03 and F-60 have 25 questions in 38 minutes, and S-95 has 35 questions in 53 minutes. F-01: Fire Guard for Impairment (Citywide)

  • One fire guard may patrol no more than 50,000 square feet of building floor area. (F-01 booklet p. 9)
  • Areas where the fire protection system is out of service must be patrolled at least once every hour, and impairment fire guards are required 24 hours a day until the systems are restored. (F-01 booklet p. 9)
  • After 4 hours of an out-of-service condition, only F-01 certificate holders may do the patrols. (F-01 booklet p. 9)
  • If there is no impairment coordinator, the building owner acts as the impairment coordinator. Records on maintaining the fire protection systems are kept at the location for three years. (F-01 booklet p. 9)
  • Fire guards make regular inspections of their area and cannot be given any other responsibilities. If no extinguisher is provided in the area, the fire guard carries one. (F-01 booklet p. 18; F-01 booklet p. 17)
  • Exit aisles are generally at least 3 feet wide, locks or chains on exit doors must be removed while the building is in use, and self-closing doors must never be left open. (F-01 booklet p. 18)
  • Hot work is not allowed where the sprinkler system is impaired; at a construction site, not if the sprinkler or standpipe system is impaired. (F-01 booklet p. 19)
  • FDNY must be notified whenever a standpipe system is or will be out of service for any period of time, or when sprinkler work cannot be finished and the system restored within 8 hours. (F-01 booklet p. 21)
  • In a fire, call 911 first, then notify the impairment coordinator, FLSD, CSFSM or other on-site personnel. A manual pull station may not send a signal to FDNY, so a 911 call must always be made. (F-01 booklet p. 22; F-01 booklet p. 23)
  • Occupants on the fire floor and the floor above are in the greatest danger and are evacuated first. (F-01 booklet p. 22)
  • A fire alarm may be reset only by an S-95 holder after being advised by an FDNY representative. (F-01 booklet p. 24)
  • Extinguishers: the top no more than 5 feet above the floor, the bottom at least 4 inches off the floor, never on the floor. Use P.A.S.S.: Pull, Aim, Squeeze, Sweep. Never use foam or water on electrical fires. (F-01 booklet p. 27; F-01 booklet p. 29)
  • Lithium-ion batteries can re-ignite minutes, hours or even days after the visible fire is out. (F-01 booklet p. 34)

F-03: Indoor Place of Assembly Safety Personnel

  • FDNY may require safety personnel where the Certificate of Occupancy shows 75 or more people gathering indoors, or 200 or more outdoors. (F-03 booklet p. 10)
  • For temporary places of assembly, safety personnel arrive at least 1 hour before the event and stay until all audience members leave. (F-03 booklet p. 10)
  • Their main duty is to “assist the occupants” in a fire or other emergency. People are likely to leave the way they came in, even if it is not the nearest or safest exit. (F-03 booklet p. 9)
  • The C-P-E check: Capacity not over the posted limit; Protection systems (smoke, fire alarm, sprinklers, extinguishers) working; Exits unblocked, unlocked and marked. (F-03 booklet p. 25)
  • Required aisles must be unobstructed, and people may not stand in them. If your area is overcrowded, tell your supervisor immediately. (F-03 booklet p. 27)
  • The audio announcement is made not more than 10 minutes before each performance. Logbooks and records stay on the premises for at least three years. (F-03 booklet p. 30)
  • If a required fire protection system is out of service, additional F-01 fire guards are required, and an F-01 holder cannot do both duties at the same time. (F-03 booklet p. 24; F-03 booklet p. 31)
  • At temporary place of assembly (TPA) events, safety personnel posted at exits must have a two-way radio, and guests may not charge or store lithium-ion batteries. (F-03 booklet p. 32; F-03 booklet p. 34)
  • Do not activate the manual fire alarm for an active shooter emergency. The three tactics are Avoid, Barricade, Confront. (F-03 booklet p. 48; F-03 booklet p. 45)
  • Class K fires involve vegetable or animal oils and fats in cooking appliances. (F-03 booklet p. 52)

F-60: Fire Guard for Torch Operations

  • F-60 fire guards do the fire watch for torch operations at construction sites, on any rooftop, and in any building when the operator holds a citywide torch permit. (F-60 booklet p. 17)
  • Cutting or welding must be at least 35 feet from combustible materials and waste; all other hot work at least 25 feet. (F-60 booklet p. 16)
  • Each person operates only one torch at a time, and an ignited torch is never left unattended. (F-60 booklet p. 17)
  • People doing a fire watch must not have other duties. On a roof, fire guards are on continuous duty during all torch operations. (F-60 booklet p. 19; F-60 booklet p. 20)
  • One fire guard may watch several torch operations if each is no more than 50 feet away by the actual path of travel. (F-60 booklet p. 20)
  • Extinguishers: at least one rated 2-A:20-B:C readily accessible for hot work; at least one multipurpose 3-A:40-B:C for roofing. (F-60 booklet p. 19)
  • The fire watch continues at least 30 minutes after hot work ends. For CNG or LPG torch work, inspections follow at 30 minutes and 1 hour after completion. (F-60 booklet p. 22)
  • Shut the gas supply to the torch completely when hot work stops for 1 hour or more. Test suspected leaks with soapy water. (F-60 booklet p. 24)
  • The hot work authorization stays available for FDNY inspection during the work and for 48 hours after. Fires of all sizes must be reported, even if put out immediately. (F-60 booklet p. 10; F-60 booklet p. 23)

S-95: Supervision of Fire Alarm Systems

  • A fire alarm system without a voice communication system must be supervised by an S-95 holder. (S-95 booklet p. 10)
  • A supervisory signal shows a need for action on guard tours or fire extinguishing systems; a trouble signal shows a fault in a monitored circuit or component. (S-95 booklet p. 14)
  • Central stations process fire alarm signals before any others, and every fire alarm signal is retransmitted to FDNY immediately, even if it restores itself. (S-95 booklet p. 17)
  • Record the date and time of each alarm, its location, the device activated and the probable cause. Never reset the system until FDNY has verified the condition. (S-95 booklet p. 19)
  • It is unlawful to transmit two or more unnecessary or unwarranted alarms in any three-month period. (S-95 booklet p. 30)
  • Before a test, inspection or drill, take the system off-line by notifying the central station. (S-95 booklet p. 42)
  • Only S-97 or S-98 holders may service and test fire alarm systems, and S-95 holders may not clean smoke detectors. Smoke detectors are cleaned at least every six months (analog detectors within a week of an indication) and tested for smoke entry at least once a year. (S-95 booklet p. 35; S-95 booklet p. 41)
  • The alarm log book is kept at the main fire alarm control panel; it is bound (not spiral), with numbered, lined pages; entries are in ink and dated; and it is kept for three years from the last entry. (S-95 booklet p. 36)
  • Manual fire alarm boxes must be red. (S-95 booklet p. 41)
