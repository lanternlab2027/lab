What we can and can’t promise

House history depends on what survives in the records. Here is what that means for your report.

Included

  • Everything the city’s open data holds for your lot: building facts, DOB and Landmarks permits, and recorded documents from 1966 onward
  • A search of the Library of Congress’s digitized newspapers for your address
  • A source link for every fact, so you can check any of it
  • A plain statement of what we looked for and didn’t find

Not included

  • Ownership before 1966. For most houses those deeds aren’t online and need in-person archive work.
  • A title search or legal opinion of any kind
  • Names of current owners, or of private individuals in recent sales
  • Newspaper coverage after the early 1920s, which is mostly outside the free archive
  • Reproductions of the 1940s city tax photos. We link to them instead, because their license covers personal use only.

Questions

Does my house need to be a landmark?

No. Landmarked houses and houses in historic districts tend to have the richest records, including an LPC designation report. Every lot in the city has building, permit and property-record data, though, and many have newspaper mentions.

Which boroughs do you cover?

All five. The city datasets cover every borough. Library of Congress Sanborn map and newspaper coverage is strongest for Manhattan and Brooklyn, and each report says what was available for your address.

What if there isn’t much on my house?

Some houses have thin records. Every report says plainly what was searched and what wasn’t found, and we never pad a report with guesses.

Why only ownership since 1966?

That is where the city’s searchable online property register (ACRIS) begins for most lots. Earlier deeds sit in bound volumes and require on-site research. Where a public source such as an LPC report or an old real-estate column names an earlier owner, we include it with a citation.

Can I give it as a gift without knowing the owner?

All we need is the address. The report is about the house, and it never names current owners.

How is it delivered?

As a print-ready digital report within 48 hours of your order. It looks good on screen and printed at home.

Are the images in the report free to use?

The Sanborn maps we reproduce come from the Library of Congress, which describes its online Sanborn collection as public domain. Other images are linked, not copied.
