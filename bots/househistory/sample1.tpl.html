The Landmarks Preservation Commission describes the house as three stories high above a basement and … faced with brownstone, its façade crowned by a bracketed and modillioned sheet metal roof cornice. [4] It is a landmark chiefly as the home for 20 years of Langston Hughes, author and poet [4]: Hughes lived and worked on the top floor from 1947 to 1967. [5]

City records note: the PLUTO tax-lot file classes the building as C5 (converted dwelling/rooming house) and lists 11 residential units. [1] That count looks high for a 2,754 sq ft rowhouse; in a paid report we would check it against the Certificate of Occupancy before relying on it.

IIThe block & neighborhood then

The LPC calls No. 20 typical of rowhouses built in Harlem during the period after the Civil War. [4] By 1896 the Sanborn fire-insurance atlas shows it as one house in an unbroken row of three-story brick dwellings, Nos. 14–30, on the south side of East 127th Street between Fifth and Madison Avenues. On the same sheet, St. Andrew’s (Protestant Episcopal) Church stands across 127th Street at the Fifth Avenue corner, a Methodist church sits on the next block east along Madison Avenue, and the elevated railroad structure runs along Park Avenue. [12]

  • 1869: Built by developers James Meagher and Thomas Hanson to designs of Alexander Wilson. [2][4]
  • 1874: A family living at No. 20 holds a child’s funeral from the house. [13]
  • 1891–94: The Mount Morris School advertises its address as 20 East 127th Street. [14][15]
  • 1906: A physician gives No. 20 as his address. [16]
  • 1920: Funeral notices name the funeral parlors of Henry Riddle at No. 20; in May the estate of George B. Goldschmidt sells the three-story dwelling. [18][19]
  • 1947: Emerson and Ethel (Toy) Harper buy the house, and Langston Hughes moves in with them, occupying the top floor as a workroom. [4]
  • 1967: Hughes’s twenty years at the house end. [2][5]
  • 1981 / 1982: Designated a NYC landmark, then listed on the National Register. [3][5]
  • 2023: A June re-opening event presents the house to the public; Hughes used the topmost floor as his work room. [20]

Landmarks Preservation Commission permits: In March 2025 the LPC issued Permit for Minor Work LPC-25-08811 (received 17 Mar, issued 28 Mar 2025). Its scope lists brownstone and areaway-ironwork replacement, repointing, repair of wood and ornamental sheet-metal features, roofing, lighting and intercom, rooftop mechanical equipment, and work to recreate missing architectural features. [8] The 2026 scaffold and shed filings are consistent with that façade work beginning, but the records don’t link the two.
